Case 01 — Independent Product

UpCycle: A Campus Clothing Marketplace

Founder · Product Manager · UX Designer · Developer

🚧 Currently Building MVP

UpCycle is a campus-exclusive marketplace that allows college students to buy, sell, and rent clothing within their university community. The idea grew from my own experience at Davidson, where students frequently borrow outfits from friends for formals, interviews, and themed events while closets remain full of clothing that is rarely worn. Existing marketplaces like Depop solve part of the problem, but they rely on shipping, lack campus verification, and aren't designed for the speed and convenience students need.

  • Problem: Students need an affordable, trustworthy way to access clothing for one-time events while giving unused items a second life.
  • Product Vision: Build a verified marketplace where students can buy, sell, or rent clothing locally using their university email, eliminating shipping costs and making exchanges simple.
  • Current Progress: Conducting user interviews, designing high-fidelity Figma prototypes, defining the MVP, and building the first version with HTML/CSS and Claude Code.
  • Next Steps: Test the MVP with Davidson students, iterate based on feedback, and expand to additional college campuses.

Case 02 — Research to Product

A social fitness tracker, built from real interviews

Needfinding · UX Research · Product Design

Fitness trackers are everywhere, but I wanted to know if they actually work for the people using them. Conducted 3 in-depth user interviews, ranging from a 19-year-old college athlete to a 33-year-old mom to a 23-year-old software engineer, and synthesized findings into empathy maps that informed product direction.

The real problem wasn't accuracy or features — it was isolation. Every participant independently said they wanted a workout partner, and every participant found their existing tracker cluttered or demotivating.

  • Insight: a platform connecting users by ability and proximity mattered more than any new tracking feature.
  • Design response: a website (not another wearable) layered onto whatever device a user already owns, with a customizable, low-clutter homepage and a "workout buddy" matching and messaging system.
  • Validation: Completed 3 usability sessions uncovering 12 actionable usability issues which reshaped the next iteration.

Case 02 — Multi-Platform Design

Redesigning a furniture retailer for three screens at once

Persona Research · Cross-Platform UI · Figma

Working in a team, I redesigned the Knotty & Board furniture website across three very different surfaces — desktop, Apple Watch, and a large multi-touch display — after building three user personas spanning a 62-year-old retiree, a shopping-with-kids mom, and an efficiency-focused recent grad.

  • Constraint-driven design: the watch interface was stripped to a single job — scan an item in-store and add it to cart — since a shopping catalog doesn't belong on a 40mm screen.
  • The large display became a collaborative, in-store design tool: a 3D/2D/POV toggle let shoppers preview furniture in their own floor plan.
  • Iteration: feedback sessions on all three prototypes drove concrete changes — from relabeling ambiguous icons to restructuring the desktop homepage navigation per instructor feedback.

Case 03 — From Screen to Physical Prototype

Taking a digital concept into the physical world

Usability Testing · Physical Prototyping · Accessibility

I extended the Knotty & Board work into a physical concept: an accessible, sensor-equipped shopping cart for in-store navigation. I wrote and ran a 9-question usability test, then translated the findings into a 3D model and a working cardboard-and-scooter prototype.

  • Testing surfaced real usability gaps — a search bar that was too visually small for how central it was to the task.
  • Accessibility became a first-class constraint: the prototype added text-to-speech and adjustable font size after usability sessions flagged issues for visually impaired shoppers.
  • Future iteration plans included wider seating for groups and self-driving navigation to reduce the accessibility burden on the shopper.

Case 04 — Data Storytelling

Turning a churn dataset into a decision-ready dashboard

Data Visualization · Analytics · Storytelling

Using a public Spotify subscription and churn dataset from Kaggle, I designed both an analytical dashboard and a persuasive, stakeholder-facing visualization — going through five rounds of design sheets to get there.

  • Ruled things out deliberately: a world map looked appealing but was mostly empty given only 8 countries in the data, so I moved to a grid and later a stacked bar chart comparing gender to subscription type.
  • Built for accessibility: encoded subscription type with both color and shape in the final scatterplot, so the chart still works for color-blind users.
  • The persuasive version centered on retention — a heatmap, a sign-up-to-churn funnel, and a survival curve — built specifically for a Spotify product/design audience rather than a general one.

Case 05 — Emerging Technology

A 360° VR experience built with AI-assisted generation

VR Prototyping · A-Frame · Prompt Engineering

With a small team, I helped build a 360° VR "time machine" through Davidson's Lake Campus, reimagining it across four seasons and three eras — past, present, and future — using AI video generation (Veo, Gemini) layered into an A-Frame environment.

  • Owned the "Summer" scene end to end, iterating on generation prompts to get a stationary 360° camera — a constraint the AI tools frequently ignored, which shaped how I wrote every later prompt.
  • Practical constraints drove decisions: limited generation tokens and inconsistent AI output meant documenting bugs transparently rather than hiding them.
  • Cross-functional collaboration: coordinated scene design and a shared GitHub repo across four teammates working independently on their own seasons.

04 — Test

How I validate ideas

Design that isn't tested is just an opinion. These are the methods I return to across every project, on paper and in practice.

Needfinding & Interviews

Structured interviews and empathy mapping to find the root problem, not the presenting one.

Usability Testing

Task-based sessions with think-aloud protocol to catch friction before it ships.

Heuristic Evaluation

Independent expert review against established usability guidelines.

Five Design Sheets

Structured ideation from low-fidelity sketches to a converged final design.

Data Visualization

Analytical and persuasive charting, built for the audience that has to act on it.

Rapid Prototyping

Paper, Figma, and physical builds — whichever gets a testable idea in front of users fastest.

AI-Assisted Workflows

Prompt engineering with generative AI tools (Veo, Gemini, ChatGPT) for rapid concept visualization.

Shared repos, divided ownership, and synthesis across teammates' independent work.
